PART I - DATASHEET DS-TSV7H REVISION 6 (EXTRACT)
1 DESCRIPTION AND ORDERING INFORMATION
1.1 The TSV7H series are 32-bit microcontrollers built around an RV32IMFC core with a single-precision floating-point unit, a 16 KB instruction cache and a 16 KB data cache. They embed up to 1024 KB of dual-bank flash memory with error-correcting code (ECC), 512 KB of SRAM, and an internal low-dropout regulator (LDO) whose output, VCORE, is set by one of four voltage-scaling ranges, VOS0 to VOS3.
1.2 The peripherals include two 12-bit successive-approximation ADCs (ADC1 and ADC2) of up to 3.57 Msps, a 12-bit DAC, two CAN FD controllers (FDCAN1 and FDCAN2), four USARTs, three SPI interfaces with I2S and TI frame modes, two I2C interfaces with SMBus and PMBus support, a CORDIC co-processor for trigonometric functions, and an advanced-control timer (TIM1) with complementary PWM outputs, dead-time insertion and break inputs.
1.3 A part number is TSV7H followed by a flash code, a package code and a temperature-grade code. Flash code 4 denotes 512 KB and 8 denotes 1024 KB. Package code Q denotes QFN-48 (7 × 7 mm, with an exposed pad) and L denotes LQFP-64 (10 × 10 mm). Temperature-grade code I denotes the industrial grade (Ta −40 to +85 °C, Tj max 105 °C), X the extended grade (Ta −40 to +105 °C, Tj max 125 °C) and A the high-temperature grade (Ta −40 to +125 °C, Tj max 140 °C).
1.4 For example, TSV7H8LA is a 1024 KB device in LQFP-64 of the high-temperature grade, and TSV7H4LI a 512 KB device in LQFP-64 of the industrial grade.
1.5 The third line of the top marking gives the revision code of the silicon, followed by a space and the date code, e.g. "B 2611". The revision codes are listed in the device identification table of the errata sheet: the revision code on a marking is not necessarily the letter of the silicon revision.

3 OPERATING CONDITIONS
3.1 The device operates with VDD from 1.71 V to 3.6 V. VDDA, the supply of the ADCs and of the DAC, operates from 1.71 V to 3.6 V and must not exceed VDD by more than 0.3 V during power-up and power-down.
3.2 The voltage-scaling range is selected in PWR_D3CR.VOS; it sets VCORE and the maximum SYSCLK frequency (Table 6). VOS0 is the overdrive range: it can be entered only from VOS1, only while the LDO supplies VCORE, and only while VDD is at least 1.95 V.

4 SUPPLY SUPERVISION
4.1 The power-on reset (POR) and the power-down reset (PDR) hold the device in reset while VDD is below their thresholds. The brown-out reset (BOR) adds a selectable threshold: BOR_LEV in the user option bytes selects level 1, 2 or 3, or level 0, which leaves only the POR and the PDR active.
Table 8. Reset thresholds
| BOR level | Rising threshold typ (V) | Falling threshold typ (V) | Falling threshold min (V) |
| Level 0, POR and PDR only | 1.68 | 1.64 | 1.60 |
| Level 1 | 2.10 | 2.00 | 1.95 |

4.2 The device leaves reset when VDD rises above the rising threshold of the selected level, and enters reset when VDD falls below its falling threshold. 5 CLOCKS
5.1 Clock sources: a high-speed external crystal oscillator (HSE) of 4 to 48 MHz, or an external clock of up to 50 MHz in bypass mode; a 64 MHz internal RC oscillator (HSI), trimmed to ±1 % at Ta = 25 °C and within ±3.5 % from −40 to +125 °C; a 4 MHz low-power internal RC oscillator (CSI); and a 32.768 kHz low-speed external crystal oscillator (LSE) for the real-time clock.
5.2 Three phase-locked loops, PLL1, PLL2 and PLL3, take their reference from HSI, CSI or HSE through a pre-divider DIVMx of 1 to 63. Each PLL has a voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO) and three output dividers, P, Q and R, each of 1 to 128. SYSCLK is taken from the P output of PLL1, from HSI, from CSI or from HSE. The P divider of PLL1 accepts only even values; the P dividers of PLL2 and PLL3 accept any value.
5.3 The PLL input frequency fREF is the reference frequency divided by DIVMx. The VCO frequency fVCO is fREF multiplied by Nx, an integer of 4 to 512, and each output frequency is fVCO divided by its output divider.
Table 10. PLL characteristics

5.5 RCC_PLLCKSELR holds the source selection and DIVM1 to DIVM3; RCC_PLLCFGR holds PLLxRGE, PLLxVCOSEL and the output enables; RCC_PLL1DIVR holds N1, P1, Q1 and R1. A PLL must be disabled before any of its fields is changed.

10 COMMUNICATION INTERFACES
10.1 Each USART supports oversampling by 16 (OVER8 = 0) and by 8 (OVER8 = 1). The baud rate is fCK / USARTDIV with oversampling by 16 and 2 × fCK / USARTDIV with oversampling by 8; it is at most fCK / 16 or fCK / 8 respectively, and never more than 12.5 Mbit/s.
10.2 The receiver timeout, enabled with RTOEN, sets RTOF when no new character is received for the number of bit periods programmed in USART_RTOR.RTO after the last stop bit.
10.3 The SPI interfaces run at up to 75 MHz as masters and 50 MHz as slaves. In TI mode the frame format follows the TI synchronous serial protocol, and the CRC is calculated over each frame.
10.4 The I2C interfaces support standard mode, fast mode and fast-mode plus (up to 1 Mbit/s), with SMBus clock-low timeouts.
10.5 FDCAN1 and FDCAN2 support CAN FD data-phase bit rates of up to 8 Mbit/s. Above 1 Mbit/s, the transmitter delay compensation (TDC) must be enabled, with its offset TDCO programmed in FDCAN_TDCR.

12 DIRECT MEMORY ACCESS AND INTERRUPTS
12.1 Two general-purpose DMA controllers, GPDMA1 and GPDMA2, of eight channels each serve the peripherals, through a request multiplexer (DMAMUX) that routes any of 115 request lines to any channel. A master DMA (MDMA) of 16 channels moves data between the AXI memories with linked-list transfers.
12.2 The platform-level interrupt controller (PLIC) handles 150 external interrupt sources with 16 priority levels, and the core-local interruptor (CLINT) provides the machine timer and the software interrupts. The interrupt latency is 12 cycles of SYSCLK from the request to the first instruction of the handler, with the caches enabled.
13 TIMERS AND WATCHDOGS
13.1 TIM1 is a 16-bit advanced-control timer with six complementary PWM outputs, a programmable dead time of up to 8 µs, two break inputs and an encoder interface. Its kernel clock is twice PCLK2 when PPRE2 is not 1.
13.2 Four 32-bit general-purpose timers (TIM2 to TIM5) and four 16-bit timers (TIM12 to TIM15) provide input capture, output compare and one-pulse modes. Two basic timers, TIM6 and TIM7, trigger the DAC.
13.3 The independent watchdog (IWDG) runs from a 32 kHz internal RC oscillator and cannot be stopped once started; the window watchdog (WWDG) runs from PCLK1.
14 REAL-TIME CLOCK AND BACKUP DOMAIN
14.1 The RTC keeps calendar time with sub-second resolution from the LSE, with two alarms, a periodic wake-up timer and 32 backup registers. The backup domain is supplied from VBAT when VDD is absent.
14.2 VBAT operates from 1.2 V to 3.6 V. The 4 KB backup SRAM is retained in VBAT mode while its regulator is enabled.
15 DEBUG AND SECURITY
15.1 The debug port provides a two-wire serial debug interface and a four-wire JTAG interface, with eight hardware breakpoints and four watchpoints. Trace data are output through a 4-bit trace port or into a 4 KB embedded trace buffer.
15.2 Readout protection has three levels: at level 0 there is no protection; at level 1 the flash cannot be read through the debug port; at level 2 the debug port is disabled permanently. A regression from level 1 to level 0 erases the user flash.
15.3 Secure boot verifies the ECDSA P-256 signature of the first-stage firmware before executing it; the hash of the public key is programmed in one-time-programmable (OTP) words.
16 PACKAGE INFORMATION
16.1 QFN-48: body of 7.00 × 7.00 mm, height of 0.85 mm max, pitch of 0.50 mm and exposed pad of 5.60 × 5.60 mm. LQFP-64: body of 10.00 × 10.00 mm, height of 1.60 mm max and pitch of 0.50 mm.
16.2 Both packages are rated at moisture sensitivity level 3 (MSL 3), with a peak reflow temperature of 260 °C, per IPC/JEDEC J-STD-020.
16.3 The exposed pad of the QFN-48 is connected to VSS and is soldered to a ground area of the board for electrical and thermal performance.

ES-01 Debug: on revision A, a breakpoint that is set while a halt request is pending is lost if the core executes WFI before the halt takes effect. Workaround: set breakpoints while the core is halted. Fixed in revision B.
ES-02 PLL: on revisions A and B, PLL1 can lose lock when Tj is below 0 °C and fVCO of PLL1 is above 836 MHz; SYSCLK then switches to HSI, and no clock-security interrupt is raised. Conditions: a PLL1 configuration with fVCO above 836 MHz, in an application whose junction temperature can fall below 0 °C. Workaround: keep fVCO of PLL1 at or below 836 MHz. PLL2 and PLL3 are not affected. Fixed in revision C.
ES-03 Flash: on revisions A and B, the read margin of the flash is insufficient in VOS1 when SYSCLK is above 360 MHz and Ta is above 85 °C. Workaround: limit SYSCLK to 360 MHz in VOS1 when Ta can exceed 85 °C, or use VOS0 where Table 6 permits it. Fixed in revision C, for which Table 6 applies without change.
ES-04 ADC: the INL of ADC1 and ADC2 can reach ±6 LSB when the sampling rate fS is above 2.0 Msps and VDDA is below 2.0 V. Conditions: both, on any channel; the DNL is not affected. Workaround: limit fS to 2.0 Msps while VDDA is below 2.0 V, or supply VDDA at 2.0 V or more. With the workaround, the INL of Table 17 applies; without it, the maximum INL is ±6 LSB. Present in all revisions.
ES-05 USART: with OVER8 = 1 and the receiver timeout enabled (RTOEN = 1), RTOF is set one bit period before the programmed timeout. Workaround: program USART_RTOR.RTO with the wanted value plus one. With OVER8 = 0 the timeout is correct. Present in all revisions.
ES-06 I2C: on revision A, the SMBus clock-low timeout is not detected in fast-mode plus. Workaround: use fast mode for SMBus traffic. Fixed in revision B.
ES-07 SPI: on revisions A and B, the CRCERR flag is not cleared at the end of a TI-mode frame and reports a CRC error for the next frame. Workaround: clear CRCERR by software after each TI-mode frame, before the next frame starts. Fixed in revision C.
ES-08 FDCAN: on revision C, the bus-off recovery sequence clears the transmitter delay compensation offset TDCO in FDCAN_TDCR, so that transmissions with a data phase above 1 Mbit/s then fail with bit errors. Conditions: TDC enabled, and a bus-off recovery, automatic or by software. Workaround: write FDCAN_TDCR again after each bus-off recovery, before INIT is cleared. Field reports of July 2026 showed that the failure stops all CAN FD traffic of the node until the workaround runs; this revision of the errata sheet therefore raises the impact class of ES-08 from 2 to 4.
ES-09 Power: on revisions B and C, after a cumulative 1 000 hours of operation at a Tj above 125 °C, the falling threshold of BOR level 3 can be up to 90 mV below the minimum of Table 8. Conditions: BOR level 3 selected, and operation at a Tj above 125 °C, which only grade A devices permit. Workaround: select BOR level 2 and supervise VDD externally. Levels 1 and 2 are not affected. Earlier documents listed ES-09 as fixed in revision C; tests of production lots of revision C showed the drift as well.
DC-1: Section 2.2 of the datasheet: on the QFN-48 package, the HBM rating of pins PB12 to PB15 is 1000 V (class 1C), not 2000 V. All other pins, and all the pins of the LQFP-64 package, are rated 2000 V.
DC-2: Table 14 of the datasheet: in VOS1, the HCLK maximum for 2 wait states is 165 MHz, not 185 MHz. The other entries of Table 14 are correct.
DC-3: Section 8.2 of the datasheet: for a QFN-48 whose exposed pad is not soldered, add 18 °C/W to θJA, not 14 °C/W.

PART IV - APPLICATION NOTE AN-7712 REVISION 2 (EXTRACT)
IV.1 AN-7712 gives guidelines for the clock and thermal design of TSV7H applications. It is informative.
IV.2 For a first thermal estimate on a typical four-layer board, a θJA of 38 °C/W can be assumed for the LQFP-64 package and of 26 °C/W for the QFN-48 package, with the typical IDD of the datasheet. A design is verified with the method of Section 8 of the datasheet.
IV.3 With a 25 MHz crystal and DIVM1 = 5, the PLL1 input frequency is 5 MHz, which requires PLL1RGE = 10. N1 = 160 and P1 = 2 then give a SYSCLK of 400 MHz.
IV.4 With HPRE = 2, a SYSCLK of 400 MHz gives an HCLK of 200 MHz, which needs 3 wait states in VOS1.
IV.5 In laboratory tests of revision B and C devices at VDDA = 1.8 V and fS = 2.57 Msps, the INL stayed within ±5 LSB, inside the limit of ES-04.
IV.6 A guard band of at least 10 °C between the computed Tj and the Tj max of the temperature grade is recommended.
IV.7 For the HSE, use a crystal with a load capacitance of 6 to 12 pF and an ESR below 60 Ω at 25 MHz, and place its load capacitors within 5 mm of the OSC_IN and OSC_OUT pins.
IV.8 Decoupling: place one 100 nF capacitor at each VDD pin and one 4.7 µF bulk capacitor; VDDA needs a 1 µF and a 10 nF capacitor in parallel, fed from VDD through a ferrite bead.
IV.9 For CAN FD at 5 Mbit/s, keep each stub shorter than 0.3 m and terminate both ends of the bus with 120 Ω.

DR-114 - SERVO DRIVE CONTROLLER HD-SC3 REVISION 3
DR-114.1 Board HD-SC3 revision 3 is a servo drive controller fitted with a TSV7H8LX. Prototype lot P1 (40 boards, built in April 2026) carries devices marked "B 2611". Production lot P2, which is the subject of this review unless an entry says otherwise, carries devices marked "Y 2636".
DR-114.2 Supply: VDD and VDDA are supplied from one 1.8 V rail with a tolerance of ±3 %. BOR level 0 is selected. VCORE runs in the voltage-scaling range VOS1.
DR-114.3 Environment: the board is mounted in a sealed drive enclosure without forced air flow, where the ambient temperature is from +5 °C to +95 °C. The board has four layers, two of which are internal ground and power planes.
DR-114.4 Clock configuration: HSE crystal of 25 MHz; DIVM1 = 5; PLL1RGE = 01; PLL1VCOSEL = 0; N1 = 140; P1 = 2; SYSCLK from the P output of PLL1; HPRE = 2; PPRE1 = 2; PPRE2 = 2; FLASH_ACR.LATENCY = 2.
DR-114.5 Peripherals: ADC1 and ADC2 convert continuously with fADC = 36 MHz from PLL2P and a sampling time of 2.5 cycles. FDCAN1 runs at 1 Mbit/s in the arbitration phase and 5 Mbit/s in the data phase, with transmitter delay compensation and automatic bus-off recovery. FDCAN2 is clocked for an expansion port but carries no traffic. USART1 is the service port, at 6 Mbit/s with OVER8 = 1 and the receiver timeout enabled. The CORDIC runs the field-oriented control loop continuously, and TIM1 drives the PWM outputs. No other peripheral is clocked.
DR-114.6 The board team computed the power of the I/O pins, PIO, as 52 mW at the highest ambient temperature.
DR-114.7 Halyard design rule HDR-7: the junction temperature computed with the method of the datasheet must be at least 20 °C below the Tj max of the temperature grade of the device. Design rule HDR-9: the firmware must implement the workaround of every erratum that is present in the silicon revision fitted and whose conditions the configuration of the board meets.
DR-114.8 Firmware release candidate FW 2.3.0 implements the workarounds of ES-02 and ES-05, and of no other erratum.
DR-114.9 On a prototype board of lot P1 at an ambient temperature of 95 °C, the on-chip temperature sensor read 104 °C after one hour at full load.
DR-114.10 EMC: the conducted emissions of the drive passed EN 55011 class A, and its radiated immunity was tested at 10 V/m.
DR-114.11 Mechanical: the board is fixed at six points, and passed the vibration test of IEC 60068-2-6 at 5 g from 10 to 500 Hz without damage.
DR-114.12 The service port of USART1 is routed to an isolated connector, which passed the IEC 61000-4-2 test at ±8 kV contact discharge.
DR-115 - SENSOR HUB HD-SH1 REVISION 1
DR-115.1 Board HD-SH1 revision 1 is an outdoor sensor hub fitted with a TSV7H4QI. All the boards built carry devices marked "B 2618".
DR-115.2 Supply: VDD and VDDA are both 3.3 V, from a regulator with a tolerance of ±2 %. BOR level 3 is selected. VCORE runs in VOS0.
DR-115.3 Environment: outdoor enclosure with natural convection, where the ambient temperature is from −25 °C to +70 °C. The board has two layers and no internal planes; the exposed pad of the device is soldered to a copper area with thermal vias.

DR-115.6 The board team computed PIO as 38 mW at the highest ambient temperature.
