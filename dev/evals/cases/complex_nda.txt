2. INFORMATION AND PROTECTION
2.1 Written, electronic and tangible information disclosed within the disclosure window is Confidential Information if marked confidential when supplied or if its confidential character would be apparent to a reasonable commercial recipient from the content and circumstances. A confidentiality legend is sufficient but is not essential under this second limb. Copies, analyses and combinations that reveal such information receive the same protection, subject to Section 3.
2.2 Oral information is protected only if the Discloser identifies it as confidential when speaking and delivers a written confirmation identifying its substance within five Business Days after the oral disclosure. Timely confirmation makes protection continuous from the oral disclosure. Until the confirmation deadline, identified oral information is provisionally protected. If confirmation arrives late, that provisional protection ends at the deadline; a later written acceptance by the Recipient restores protection prospectively from acceptance, without covering the intervening gap. Merely receiving the late confirmation is not acceptance, and silence is not consent. The apparent-confidentiality limb in Section 2.1 does not override this oral-information rule.
2.3 For this Agreement, a Business Day is a Monday through Friday other than 26 March, 29 March, 25 November and 26 November 2027. There are no other excluded dates, including dates in 2028. Count a period in Business Days starting with the first eligible day after the triggering day. A deadline expressed in days expires at 17:00 UTC on its final day. A calendar-day period likewise excludes its trigger date. Continuous hours and minutes run through nights, weekends and excluded dates. All case times are UTC.
2.4 A written communication is delivered when it reaches the designated recipient mailbox, not when sent, prepared, uploaded or opened. A notice or confirmation arriving after 17:00 UTC is treated as delivered on the next Business Day at 09:00 UTC, except that Section 6 compelled-disclosure notices are measured at actual arrival, at any hour. An automated delivery receipt proves arrival but does not express acceptance of terms.
2.5 Ordinary Confidential Information remains protected until the later of three years after the Effective Date and two years after the applicable disclosure. Trade secrets remain protected while they retain their stipulated trade-secret status. A Section 3 exclusion ends protection of the exact information covered by that exclusion as soon as the exclusion is established, even if a duration in this paragraph would otherwise extend longer. Duration labels cannot convert non-confidential information into Confidential Information.
2.6 A later resend of identical information does not start a fresh disclosure date. A separately identified substantive addition has its own disclosure date and is tested independently. Expiration of the disclosure window, a permitted disclosure to an adviser and compliance with a compulsory order do not by themselves make information public or extinguish surviving restrictions.
3. EXCLUSIONS AND EVIDENCE
3.1 The Recipient bears the burden of establishing an exclusion with contemporaneous records identifying the exact information at issue. Exclusions apply to information demonstrably known without restriction before receipt, information independently developed without using Confidential Information, information lawfully received from a third party free of a duty of confidence, and information publicly available other than through a breach by the Recipient or anyone to whom it gave access. A bare assertion, approximate match or later reconstructed recollection is insufficient.
3.2 Public or previously known components do not exclude a non-public selection, ordering, parameterisation or combination of those components. An exclusion established for a component covers that component only. Hashes identify exact versions in the appended record; a different hash is not proof of prior possession of the disclosed version. A dated, isolated development record can establish independent development even though a separate team in the same company has received Confidential Information, provided the record establishes no access or transfer between those teams before completion.
3.3 A third-party licence proves a lawful source only if the third party was free to disclose the particular information. The Recipient cannot rely on publication caused by its own breach or the breach of a permitted recipient. A later, independent and authorised public release may establish the public-information exclusion from its own release date. Only the information actually released is covered.
3.4 An exclusion for one asset is not an exclusion for another, even if a slide deck groups them together or a business sponsor calls them one workstream. A compulsory disclosure to a closed regulator is not a publication to the public. The appended asset record is exhaustive for the facts needed to apply these exclusions.

5. RESTRICTED USE AND RESIDUAL KNOWLEDGE
5.1 The Recipient must use Confidential Information only for the Permitted Purpose, protect it with reasonable care, limit copies to that Purpose and keep an access record. It may not reverse engineer supplied prototypes, reconstruct source code from observations, or train, fine-tune or evaluate a machine-learning model using Confidential Information or derivatives that reveal it without a separate signed licence. No such licence is included in this Agreement.
5.2 General unaided skills and experience retained in ordinary memory may be used after the evaluation. This residual-knowledge permission excludes deliberate memorisation, recorded notes, source code, quantitative parameters, trade secrets and material reconstructed from any of those sources. It does not authorise an act independently prohibited by Section 5.1. Calling notes a personal aide-memoire or a model a statistical summary does not change these limits.
5.3 A contractual right to retain an archival or backup copy is a custody exception only. It does not revive evaluation rights, create a development licence, authorise model training or remove the surviving confidentiality restrictions. No inference of a licence arises from the Discloser's failure to request deletion earlier.
6. COMPELLED DISCLOSURE
6.1 If binding legal process requires disclosure, the Recipient may disclose only the information counsel identifies as legally required after available narrowing and privilege claims have been resolved. It must seek confidential treatment, preserve privileged and out-of-scope material, and use the closed delivery channel required by the order. An attachment's inclusion in the initial request does not establish that all of it remains required after a narrowing order.
6.2 When notification is legally permitted at the time of service, the Recipient must notify the Discloser at least 24 continuous hours before the required production time, or immediately on service if service leaves less than 24 hours. When notice is initially prohibited, that rule is replaced by a deadline at the earlier of six continuous hours after the prohibition is lifted and the required production time. Do not subtract 24 hours from production in this second situation. The notice must actually arrive by the applicable deadline; Section 2.4's after-hours deferral does not apply.
6.3 The notice must identify the process, the final required scope, the production time and any remaining restriction on communications. It must exclude information the prohibition still covers. A ban on notifying the Discloser is not a ban on consulting the Recipient's own counsel unless the order expressly says so. The appended order states the complete scope and timing of the ban.
6.4 A permitted compulsory disclosure is not a confidentiality breach, but it neither grants a public-use licence nor establishes the Section 3 public-information exclusion. The Recipient must continue protecting retained and undisclosed material. The deadline for production and the deadline for contractual notice are separate obligations.
7. RETURN, DELETION AND LIMITED RETENTION
7.1 On the earlier of delivery of a written return request and effective termination of the disclosure window, the Recipient must cease evaluation use and return or delete ordinary active copies within ten Business Days after that trigger. A later trigger does not restart the period. A notice may request return without ending the disclosure window. Sections 7.2 and 7.3 are the only exceptions to deletion for Confidential Information in the appended custody record.
7.2 One segregated archival copy may be retained by external counsel only to establish compliance or defend a dispute, with access confined to that counsel. An immutable disaster-recovery backup may remain until the earlier of its next scheduled overwrite and 45 calendar days after the Section 7.1 trigger; restoration does not restart either clock. Restored working copies are active copies subject to the original ten-Business-Day deadline. Archive and backup copies may not be used for evaluation, product work or training.
7.3 An identified copy subject to a binding preservation order received before its deletion deadline must be preserved until the order is released. The exception extends only to the exact copies or records identified by the order and imposes no general hold on a whole project. When the order is released, delete that material within five Business Days after release unless its ordinary deletion deadline is later. A courtesy request, draft order or proposed hold is not binding process.
7.4 A deletion certificate must state the actual completion date and identify the remaining permitted archive, backup and held material with the applicable restrictions. It may not state that every copy was destroyed while an exception is being used. A transfer into a folder named Archive does not turn a working copy into the one counsel-held copy permitted by Section 7.2.
