DATA PROCESSING AGREEMENT
This Data Processing Agreement (the "DPA") is entered into as of 1 March 2026 (the "Effective Date") by and between:
Aurelia Retail Group AS, a company incorporated under the laws of Norway with registration number 998 877 665, having its registered office at Storgata 42, 0182 Oslo, Norway (the "Controller"); and
Falkenwald Data Systems GmbH, a company incorporated under the laws of Germany, registered with the commercial register of the local court of Berlin (Charlottenburg) under HRB 234567 B, having its registered office at Lindenstrasse 118, 10969 Berlin, Germany (the "Processor").
The Controller and the Processor are each referred to as a "Party" and together as the "Parties".
RECITALS
(A) The Parties have entered into a Master Services Agreement dated 15 January 2026 (the "MSA") under which the Processor provides the Controller with a hosted e-commerce analytics platform (the "Services").
(B) In the course of providing the Services, the Processor processes personal data on behalf of the Controller.
(C) The Parties enter into this DPA to satisfy the requirements of Article 28(3) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (the "GDPR").
1. DEFINITIONS
1.1 Capitalised terms not otherwise defined in this DPA have the meanings given to them in the MSA.
1.2 "Personal Data", "Data Subject", "Processing", "Personal Data Breach", "Supervisory Authority" and "Technical and Organisational Measures" have the meanings given in the GDPR.
1.3 "Sub-processor" means any third party engaged by the Processor to process Personal Data on behalf of the Controller.
2. SUBJECT MATTER AND DURATION
2.1 This DPA governs the Processing of Personal Data by the Processor on behalf of the Controller in connection with the Services.
2.2 This DPA takes effect on the Effective Date and remains in force for as long as the Processor processes Personal Data on behalf of the Controller under the MSA.
2.3 The subject matter, nature and purpose of the Processing, the types of Personal Data and the categories of Data Subjects are set out in Annex 1.
3. INSTRUCTIONS OF THE CONTROLLER
3.1 The Processor shall process Personal Data only on documented instructions from the Controller, including with regard to transfers of Personal Data to a third country, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law to which the Processor is subject.
3.2 If the Processor is required by law to process Personal Data beyond the Controller's instructions, it shall inform the Controller of that legal requirement before Processing, unless that law prohibits such information on important grounds of public interest.
3.3 The Processor shall immediately inform the Controller if, in its opinion, an instruction infringes the GDPR or other Union or Member State data protection provisions.
4. CONFIDENTIALITY
4.1 The Processor shall ensure that persons authorised to process the Personal Data have committed themselves to confidentiality or are under an appropriate statutory obligation of confidentiality.
4.2 The confidentiality obligations under this Section 4 survive the termination of this DPA.
5. SECURITY OF PROCESSING
5.1 Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation and the nature, scope, context and purposes of Processing, the Processor shall implement and maintain the Technical and Organisational Measures set out in Annex 2.
5.2 The Processor shall ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, including, as appropriate, pseudonymisation and encryption of Personal Data, the ability to ensure ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability and resilience of processing systems, and a process for regularly testing, assessing and evaluating the effectiveness of the measures.
5.3 The Processor may update the Technical and Organisational Measures from time to time, provided that such updates do not materially reduce the overall level of protection.
6. SUB-PROCESSING
6.1 The Controller grants the Processor a general authorisation to engage Sub-processors for the Processing of Personal Data, subject to this Section 6.
6.2 The Sub-processors approved as of the Effective Date are listed in Annex 3.
6.3 The Processor shall inform the Controller in writing of any intended addition or replacement of a Sub-processor at least 30 days before the change takes effect, thereby giving the Controller the opportunity to object to such changes.
6.4 If the Controller objects on reasonable data protection grounds within 30 days of the notice, the Parties shall discuss the objection in good faith; if no resolution is reached, the Controller may terminate the affected part of the Services without penalty.
6.5 The Processor shall impose on each Sub-processor, by way of a written contract, data protection obligations that are no less protective than those set out in this DPA, and the Processor remains fully liable to the Controller for the performance of each Sub-processor's obligations.
7. ASSISTANCE WITH DATA SUBJECT RIGHTS
7.1 Taking into account the nature of the Processing, the Processor shall assist the Controller by appropriate technical and organisational measures, insofar as this is possible, for the fulfilment of the Controller's obligation to respond to requests for exercising the Data Subject's rights under Chapter III of the GDPR.
7.2 The Processor shall notify the Controller of any request received directly from a Data Subject within 5 business days of receipt and shall not respond to such request itself, except on the documented instructions of the Controller.
7.3 The Processor shall provide the assistance described in this Section 7 at no additional charge, unless the volume of requests is manifestly excessive.
8. PERSONAL DATA BREACH NOTIFICATION
8.1 The Processor shall notify the Controller without undue delay, and in any event no later than 48 hours, after becoming aware of a Personal Data Breach affecting Personal Data processed under this DPA.
8.2 The notification under Section 8.1 shall, at a minimum, describe the nature of the Personal Data Breach, the categories and approximate number of Data Subjects and Personal Data records concerned, the likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed to address the breach and mitigate its possible adverse effects.
8.3 The Processor shall document all Personal Data Breaches and shall cooperate with the Controller in relation to any notifications to Supervisory Authorities or communications to Data Subjects that the Controller determines are required.
9. DATA PROTECTION IMPACT ASSESSMENTS
9.1 The Processor shall provide reasonable assistance to the Controller with any data protection impact assessments and prior consultations with Supervisory Authorities which the Controller reasonably considers to be required by Articles 35 and 36 of the GDPR, in each case solely in relation to the Processing of Personal Data under this DPA.
10. AUDITS AND INSPECTIONS
10.1 The Processor shall make available to the Controller all information necessary to demonstrate compliance with the obligations laid down in this DPA and shall allow for and contribute to audits, including inspections, conducted by the Controller or an auditor mandated by the Controller.
10.2 The Controller may conduct an on-site audit no more than once per calendar year, upon at least 30 days' prior written notice, during normal business hours and without unreasonably interfering with the Processor's business operations.
10.3 Notwithstanding Section 10.2, the Controller may audit the Processor at any time following a Personal Data Breach or where required by a Supervisory Authority.
10.4 Each Party shall bear its own costs in connection with an audit under Section 10.2.
11. INTERNATIONAL TRANSFERS
11.1 The Processor shall not transfer Personal Data to a country outside the European Economic Area unless the transfer is subject to appropriate safeguards under Chapter V of the GDPR.
11.2 Where a transfer is based on standard contractual clauses adopted by the European Commission, the Parties shall execute such clauses, and this DPA shall be interpreted so as not to conflict with them.
11.3 As of the Effective Date, all Personal Data is stored in data centres located in Norway and Estonia.
12. RETURN AND DELETION OF PERSONAL DATA
12.1 Upon termination or expiry of the MSA, the Processor shall, at the choice of the Controller, delete or return all Personal Data to the Controller within 90 days, and delete existing copies unless Union or Member State law requires storage of the Personal Data.
12.2 Upon the Controller's request, the Processor shall certify in writing that it has complied with Section 12.1.
13. LIABILITY
13.1 Each Party's aggregate liability arising out of or in connection with this DPA is subject to the limitations and exclusions of liability set out in the MSA, save that the aggregate liability of the Processor for breaches of this DPA shall not exceed EUR 500,000 or the total fees paid by the Controller under the MSA during the 12 months preceding the event giving rise to the claim, whichever is higher.
13.2 Nothing in this DPA limits either Party's liability for damages suffered by Data Subjects as provided under Article 82 of the GDPR.
14. TERM AND TERMINATION
14.1 This DPA commences on the Effective Date and terminates automatically upon expiry or termination of the MSA, subject to Section 12.
14.2 Sections 4, 12 and 13 survive the termination of this DPA.
15. GOVERNING LAW AND JURISDICTION
15.1 This DPA is governed by the laws of Norway, without regard to its conflict of laws principles.
15.2 The courts of Oslo, Norway have exclusive jurisdiction to settle any dispute arising out of or in connection with this DPA.
ANNEX 1 - DETAILS OF PROCESSING
Subject matter of the Processing: provision of a hosted e-commerce analytics platform, including collection, storage, and analysis of customer interaction data.
Duration of the Processing: the term of the MSA plus the retention period set out in Section 12.
Nature and purpose of the Processing: hosting, storage, analysis, reporting, and technical support.
Types of Personal Data: full name, email address, phone number, order history, IP address, device identifiers, approximate location derived from IP address.
Categories of Data Subjects: customers of the Controller, prospective customers who have created user accounts, and employees of the Controller who use the platform's administrative interface.
Special categories of Personal Data: none; the Controller shall not submit special categories of Personal Data to the Services.
ANNEX 2 - TECHNICAL AND ORGANISATIONAL MEASURES
Encryption of Personal Data in transit using TLS 1.3 and at rest using AES-256.
Multi-factor authentication for all administrative access to production systems.
Role-based access controls with access reviews conducted at least quarterly.
Logging and monitoring of access to production systems, with logs retained for 12 months.
Daily encrypted backups with a maximum retention of 35 days and documented restoration testing twice per year.
Annual penetration testing performed by an independent third party.
Mandatory annual data protection and information security training for all personnel with access to Personal Data.
A documented incident response plan reviewed at least annually.
ANNEX 3 - APPROVED SUB-PROCESSORS
Skylark Hosting OU, registered in Estonia, providing cloud infrastructure and data centre services from facilities located in Tallinn, Estonia.
Meridian Mail Ltd, registered in Ireland, providing transactional email delivery services from facilities located in Dublin, Ireland.
Baltika Analytics UAB, registered in Lithuania, providing log aggregation and monitoring services from facilities located in Vilnius, Lithuania.
AMENDMENT NO. 1 TO THE DATA PROCESSING AGREEMENT
This Amendment No. 1 (the "Amendment") to the Data Processing Agreement dated 1 March 2026 (the "DPA") is entered into on 15 September 2026 by and between Aurelia Retail Group AS (the "Controller") and Falkenwald Data Systems GmbH (the "Processor").
AMENDMENT RECITALS
(A) The Parties entered into the DPA in connection with the Master Services Agreement dated 15 January 2026.
(B) Following a review of the Processor's incident response capabilities and planned changes to the Processor's supply chain, the Parties wish to amend the DPA as set out in this Amendment.
A1. EFFECTIVENESS AND PRECEDENCE
A1.1 This Amendment takes effect on 1 October 2026 (the "Amendment Effective Date"), save that Section A4 (Sub-processor Changes) takes effect on 1 January 2027.
A1.2 Except as expressly amended by this Amendment, the DPA remains in full force and effect.
A1.3 In the event of any conflict between this Amendment and the DPA, this Amendment prevails. In the event of any conflict between the main body of this Amendment and any annex to this Amendment, the main body of this Amendment prevails.
A2. BREACH NOTIFICATION
A2.1 Section 8.1 of the DPA is deleted and replaced with the following: "8.1 The Processor shall notify the Controller without undue delay, and in any event no later than 24 hours, after becoming aware of a Personal Data Breach affecting Personal Data processed under this DPA."
A2.2 For the avoidance of doubt, the notification content requirements in Section 8.2 of the DPA remain unchanged.
A3. AUDITS
A3.1 The notice period for on-site audits under Section 10.2 of the DPA is reduced from 30 days to 20 days.
A3.2 The audit protocol set out in Annex A-1 to this Amendment applies to all on-site audits conducted after the Amendment Effective Date.
A4. SUB-PROCESSOR CHANGES
A4.1 With effect from 1 January 2027, the approval of Meridian Mail Ltd is withdrawn, and Meridian Mail Ltd shall cease to be an approved Sub-processor.
A4.2 With effect from 1 January 2027, the following are added as approved Sub-processors: Cendrillon Mail SAS, registered in France, providing transactional email delivery services from facilities located in Paris, France; and Dagmar Backup ApS, registered in Denmark, providing encrypted backup storage services from facilities located in Copenhagen, Denmark.
A4.3 Annex 3 to the DPA is deemed amended accordingly with effect from 1 January 2027.
A5. RETURN AND DELETION
A5.1 The period of 90 days in Section 12.1 of the DPA is replaced with 60 days.
A6. LIABILITY
A6.1 Section 13.1 of the DPA is deleted and replaced with the following: "13.1 The aggregate liability of the Processor for breaches of this DPA shall not exceed EUR 1,000,000; provided that this cap shall not apply to breaches of Section 4 (Confidentiality) of the DPA."
A7. MISCELLANEOUS
A7.1 This Amendment is governed by the laws of Norway.
A7.2 This Amendment may be executed in counterparts, each of which constitutes an original.
ANNEX A-1 - AUDIT PROTOCOL
On-site audits shall be conducted during normal business hours by no more than three auditors.
The Controller shall provide at least 10 Business Days' notice of an on-site audit.
The Processor shall make relevant records available to the auditors in a secure data room.
A summary of audit findings shall be shared with the Processor within 15 days of the audit.
AMENDMENT NO. 2 - OPERATIONAL EXCEPTIONS
B1. EFFECTIVENESS, PRECEDENCE AND CLOCKS
B1.1 The Parties sign this Amendment No. 2 on 20 February 2027. It takes effect on 1 March 2027 and has no retrospective effect. Its main body prevails over both earlier instruments and every operational schedule. Earlier provisions continue where not expressly changed. The records below stipulate the complete facts for their named cases; they do not themselves amend contractual rules.
B1.2 All timestamps below are UTC. A calendar-day period excludes the triggering date and includes its final date, without moving a weekend deadline. A Business Day is Monday through Friday, except the contractual closure dates 12 April 2027 and 30 August 2027. There are no other excluded weekdays for the cases below. Business-day periods exclude the triggering date and expire at 17:00 on the final counted day. Hour periods run continuously through weekends and closures.
B2. INCIDENT CLOCKS
B2.1 The 24-hour outer limit in A2.1 remains the standard initial-notification limit. For a breach confirmed to expose usable privileged credentials, the expedited outer limit is 12 hours from the original awareness of the breach, even if the credential exposure is confirmed later. An automated alert alone is not awareness; verified unauthorised access to Personal Data is awareness. Neither a change of incident classification nor later escalation restarts either clock. The duty to notify without undue delay remains; meeting an outer limit alone does not establish compliance with that separate duty.
B2.2 A completed follow-up report is due by the end of the third Business Day after awareness. The initial notice and follow-up report are separate obligations; an incomplete report does not postpone initial notification. The content requirements in Section 8.2 and the cooperation duty in Section 8.3 continue to apply.
B2.3 Only a Controller-approved pause can suspend the follow-up clock. Exclude only the whole Business Days expressly approved in writing, even if the Processor requested a longer interval. No pause suspends the initial-notification clock. Ignore a pause request that was refused or never approved.
B3. DELETION AND LIMITED RETENTION
B3.1 For terminations from 1 March 2027 onward, A5.1's ordinary deletion period remains applicable. A legal hold applies only to the identified records, including their copies. If several holds cover the same records, release occurs only when the last applicable hold ends. Delete those held records by the later of the ordinary deletion deadline and the end of the tenth Business Day after that release. A hold does not extend deadlines for any other records.
B3.2 A backup containing no held records must be deleted by the earlier of its ordinary deletion deadline and 35 calendar days after its creation. A restore or an internal request to retain it does not restart this period. Section B3 controls in case of conflict with Annex 2's retention schedules.
B3.3 After termination, records retained solely under a legal hold may be used only for legal preservation, not service delivery or analytics. An internal request is not a Controller instruction and cannot extend a hold or authorise another use. After the ordinary deletion deadline, the hold and the deletion window following its release remain the sole retention authority for the held records.
B4. CONDITIONAL SUB-PROCESSOR AUTHORISATION
B4.1 For proposed additions or expanded processing from 1 March 2027 onward, Sections 6.3 and 6.4's notice process alone does not authorise the change. Approval begins no earlier than the proposed start date, completion of the security review, and written Controller consent, whichever is latest. All three conditions must be satisfied for the specific service and processing country. A consent limited to one service or country does not authorise another. Silence is not consent. Previously approved services remain approved unless expressly withdrawn.
B4.2 Identify a legal entity by its registration number. A trading name or corporate name change without a new registration number is not another Sub-processor. Count a country as a data location only when actual processing there and effective authorisation for it are both established as of the queried date. A registered office, proposed location, or expired emergency permission does not by itself qualify.
B4.3 The Controller can grant express emergency permission limited to its stated incident, purpose and interval. Such permission overrides an earlier withdrawal only within that scope and does not authorise ordinary customer email or processing after its expiry. Permanent approvals for other entities are unaffected.
B5. LIABILITY ALLOCATION FOR EVENTS FROM 1 MARCH 2027
B5.1 The ordinary cap is EUR 1,000,000 per Contract Year. Breaches of Sections 5 or 8 instead share one separate security cap per Contract Year, equal to the greater of EUR 2,000,000 and twice Qualifying Fees for the event. A Contract Year runs from 1 March through the last day of the following February. Qualifying Fees are recurring service fees paid in the twelve complete calendar months preceding the event's month, excluding VAT, service credits and one-off training. A paid credit reduces the fees for its stated service period. Do not include fees for the event's month. The ledger amounts below are exhaustive and all in EUR.
B5.2 A payment on an earlier claim consumes only its applicable pool for the same Contract Year. Confidentiality breaches remain uncapped under A6.1 and consume neither capped pool. An economic loss recoverable under more than one section is counted once, with confidentiality taking precedence over security, and security over ordinary liability. Distinct economic losses remain separate even if they arise from the same incident. Payments in an earlier Contract Year do not consume the current year's pools. There are no other limits, offsets, claims or payments applicable to the cases below; all stated new claims are otherwise established and payable subject only to these rules.
